## Live cloud-API arm (added 2026-10-16)

24 real calls (2 models × 2 scenarios × 2 arms × 3 reps, temperature 0, Together API, usage from the provider's own meter):

| Scenario | Arm | Qwen3.8-2.4T in-tokens | Llama-3.3-70B in-tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2 multi-doc | raw (all docs) | 67,432 | 47,886 |
| S2 multi-doc | Qāfiya (selected) | 20,810 | 15,002 |
| **Saving** | | **69.1%** | **68.7%** |

→ The offline prediction (69.2%) **holds on the provider's meter, on two different models with two different tokenizers** (Llama tokenizes the same text ~30% cheaper; the *ratio* is tokenizer-independent, exactly as plan §4 predicted).

**Surprise finding (H4, upgraded):** both models failed **every** in-head aggregation check in **both** arms (0/12 each): asked to total hundreds of rows, they hallucinate plausible-but-wrong sums whether given raw files or structured data. Conclusion for the paper: *totals must be computed by bindings, not generated by the model* — the strongest possible motivation for the derivation layer. Accuracy protocol note: with a focus prompt, both arms face the same impossible arithmetic; Qāfiya's production path serves pre-computed, Layer-1-verified aggregates instead.

**Operational notes (D5):** the two reasoning-mode models tested (Qwen3.5-9B; Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B in thinking mode) consumed their entire completion budget (8k, then 24k tokens) on every large tabular prompt without emitting an answer — 24/24 calls. The frozen chain-of-thought (`results/raw/live`, Qwen3.8 thinking-mode responses) shows row-by-row record enumeration cut at the budget (`finish_reason: length`). With reasoning disabled, or on short prompts, the same models answered immediately. Larger budgets are untested; claim is bounded to the tested window. Qwen final runs used `reasoning: {enabled: false}`. Wall-clock is reported for Llama only (Qwen ran parallel): Qāfiya arm faster (6.8s vs 9.4s median on S2) but network noise dominates — tokens remain the primary metric.

## Scope decisions (logged)

- **D1** Strata covered: tabular (6) + prose (4) + text-layer PDF (4, added 2026-10-16; same prose content in a PDF container). Scanned stratum deferred — the VLM importer is not wired; its absence is a known limitation, and scanned docs remain the only place `T_raw ≫ T_data` is plausible (page-image tokens vs extracted text).
- **D2** `T_ingest = 0` by construction for CSV/MD (deterministic, no model). Disclosed per plan §8; VLM strata would carry a real ingest cost.
- **D3** `T_out` not measured offline (equal across arms by design; cancels in k*). To be captured from provider `usage` at the live run.
- **D4** Three serving variants measured: full inline provenance, slim JSONL, compact CSV. Compact CSV is the prescribed serving form: provenance stays on disk, addressable by record id (spec §1.2 — views reference bindings, never inline values).

## Results (from frozen counts — no model calls were made)

**Single document (deck from one doc):**

| Stratum | CSV serving | JSONL serving | Full inline provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tabular (n=6) | 1.21× raw | 2.32× raw | 8.54× raw |
| Prose (n=4) | 1.15× raw | 1.15× raw | 2.32× raw |
| Text-layer PDF (n=4, added 2026-10-16) | 1.12× raw | 1.12× raw | 2.25× raw |

→ **H1/H2 falsified for single docs, now including text-PDFs.** Raw CSV is already near-minimal; a text-PDF's baseline is its own text layer, so parity there too. The only stratum where a single-doc win remains plausible is **scanned/image documents**, where the baseline must send page images (orders of magnitude more tokens than extracted text) — that measurement is gated on the VLM importer and a model budget.

**Multi-doc selective loading (H3 scenario):** deck about *travel* spend across all 6 tabular datasets:

| Arm | Input tokens |
|---|---|
| Baseline (all raw docs) | 67,678 |
| Qāfiya (filtered records only) | 20,851 |
| **Saving** | **69.2%** |

→ **H3 supported, on both granularities.** Records (tabular, 6 datasets, topic=travel): 67,678 → 20,851 tokens, **69.2% saving**. Blocks (prose + text-PDF, 8 documents, topic=travel, added 2026-10-16): 28,838 → 11,818 tokens, **59.0% saving** (64 of 156 blocks selected). The efficiency claim survives only in its selective-loading form: the win comes from *reading the slice, not the pile*, and grows with corpus size and query selectivity.

## Consequences for the paper (honest reframing)

1. Reframe the efficiency claim: *not* "cheaper per generation on one doc" but "**selective loading beats full-context as corpora grow**" + verification baked in at no extra prompt cost.
2. Design rule now backed by evidence: **provenance must be addressable, never inlined** — inlining it costs 8.5×.
3. The PDF/scanned strata remain the open question where single-doc wins may exist (raw PDF tokens are large); gate on the PDF importer.
4. Live-backend runs (cloud `usage` + local MLX) are still needed for T_out, wall-clock, and dollar figures — token-side input measurement is complete and frozen.
